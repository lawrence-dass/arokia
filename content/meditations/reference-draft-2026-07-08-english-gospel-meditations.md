## Theological Guardrails

- Christ-centered and strictly biblical.
- Every spiritual claim should be grounded in Scripture.
- The app is a tool that points people to Jesus, not a replacement for Him, His church, Scripture, or prayer.
- No prosperity gospel, word-of-faith, manifestation, self-deification, or empty-mind mindfulness.
- This is Christian contemplation: filling the heart with Christ and Scripture, resting and abiding in Him.

## Script 1: Come Unto Me

**practice_path:** body  
**mood/theme:** rest  
**title:** Come Unto Me

### 1. Welcome / Settle

Welcome. This time is not a replacement for Jesus, Scripture, prayer, or the church. It is a quiet tool to help you turn toward Christ, who says, "Come unto me" (Matthew 11:28).  
Sit or lie down in a way that is steady and humble before the Lord. [PAUSE 3s]

### 2. Breath / Body

Notice your breath. Do not empty your mind. Bring your attention to Jesus. Your life and breath are held by God, who "giveth to all life, and breath, and all things" (Acts 17:25).  
Let your shoulders soften. Let your hands rest open. Your body belongs to the Lord (1 Corinthians 6:19-20). [PAUSE 3s]  
Breathe in slowly. Breathe out slowly. Receive this moment as a place to listen.

### 3. Scripture

Matthew 11:28-30:

"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.  
Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.  
For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

[PAUSE 3s]

### 4. Reflection

Jesus does not call the weary to perform for Him. He calls them to come to Him (Matthew 11:28).  
If your body feels tired, if your mind feels crowded, if your soul feels heavy, do not pretend before Him. He already knows what you need before you ask (Matthew 6:8).

Christ's rest is not escape from obedience. He says, "Take my yoke upon you" (Matthew 11:29). His way is real discipleship, but He is "meek and lowly in heart" (Matthew 11:29).  
So bring Him the burdens you are carrying: fear, responsibility, grief, hidden sin, weariness, unanswered questions. [PAUSE 3s]

You are not asked to save yourself. Salvation belongs to the Lord (Psalm 3:8). Jesus is the one who gives rest to the soul (Matthew 11:29).  
Stay here with Him. Let the truth be simple: Christ is gentle. Christ calls. Christ gives rest.

### 5. Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, I come to You because You have invited the weary and heavy laden (Matthew 11:28).  
Teach me Your way. Make me meek and lowly in heart with You (Matthew 11:29).  
Forgive my sins, cleanse what is false in me, and lead me in Your truth (1 John 1:9; John 14:6).  
Let my rest be found in You, not in control, comfort, or distraction (Matthew 11:28-30). Amen.

### 6. Rest in His Presence

Now remain quietly before Jesus. Do not force a feeling. Do not search for a vision. Rest in the words He has spoken (Matthew 11:28-30). [PAUSE 3s]  
Inhale: "Come unto me."  
Exhale: "I will give you rest." [PAUSE 3s]

### 7. Close / Blessing

Go slowly. Christ is not hurried with the weary. He is meek and lowly in heart (Matthew 11:29).  
May you walk today under His gentle yoke and find rest for your soul.

**Scripture citations:** Matthew 11:28-30; Acts 17:25; 1 Corinthians 6:19-20; Matthew 6:8; Psalm 3:8; 1 John 1:9; John 14:6.  
**Review notes:** No unanchored doctrinal themes included. Uses breath only as embodied attentiveness to Christ, not secular empty-mind mindfulness.

## Script 2: My Peace I Give

**practice_path:** mind  
**mood/theme:** anxious  
**title:** My Peace I Give

### 1. Welcome / Settle

Welcome. This practice is only a guide to help you turn to Jesus. Peace comes from Christ Himself, not from this app (John 14:27).  
Let your body become still. Let your attention come before the Lord. [PAUSE 3s]

### 2. Breath / Body

Take a slow breath. Name before God what is troubling you. Jesus says the Father knows what you need before you ask Him (Matthew 6:8).  
Feel your feet or the surface beneath you. You are not holding yourself together by your own strength; God is "our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble" (Psalm 46:1). [PAUSE 3s]

### 3. Scripture

John 14:27:

"Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you.  
Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid."

[PAUSE 3s]

### 4. Reflection

Jesus spoke these words before suffering, not from a life without trouble (John 14:27; John 16:33). His peace is not denial. His peace is His own presence and promise.  
He tells troubled hearts not to be afraid because He is the way to the Father (John 14:6), because He does not leave His people comfortless (John 14:18), and because the Holy Spirit teaches and reminds His disciples of His words (John 14:26).

Anxiety often asks, "What if I am alone?" Jesus answers, "I will come to you" (John 14:18).  
Anxiety asks, "What if this world overcomes me?" Jesus says, "I have overcome the world" (John 16:33). [PAUSE 3s]

Do not rebuke yourself for feeling afraid. Bring fear into the light of Christ. The Lord is near to the brokenhearted (Psalm 34:18).  
Let your mind return to one truth: Jesus gives peace differently than the world gives (John 14:27).

### 5. Prayer

Lord Jesus, my heart is troubled, and You know it (John 14:27; Matthew 6:8).  
Give me the peace You promised, not the peace of denial, control, or escape, but Your peace (John 14:27).  
Holy Spirit, remind me of the words of Christ (John 14:26).  
Father, hold me in Your care. Teach me to seek first Your kingdom and righteousness today (Matthew 6:33). Amen.

### 6. Rest in His Presence

Rest now with Christ's words.  
"Let not your heart be troubled." [PAUSE 3s]  
"Neither let it be afraid." [PAUSE 3s]  
Let these words fill the heart with Christ, not emptiness.

### 7. Close / Blessing

As you return to the day, do not trust the strength of your own calm. Trust the Lord who gives peace (John 14:27).  
May the peace of Christ guard your next step.

**Scripture citations:** John 14:27; Matthew 6:8; Psalm 46:1; John 16:33; John 14:6; John 14:18; John 14:26; Psalm 34:18; Matthew 6:33.  
**Review notes:** No prosperity or "claim your outcome" framing. Peace is presented as Christ's promise amid trouble, not a guarantee of easy circumstances.

## Script 3: Abide In Me

**practice_path:** soul  
**mood/theme:** lonely  
**title:** Abide In Me

### 1. Welcome / Settle

Welcome. This is a quiet space to turn toward Jesus, who calls His disciples to abide in Him (John 15:4).  
You are not here to become self-sufficient. Jesus says, "without me ye can do nothing" (John 15:5). [PAUSE 3s]

### 2. Breath / Body

Let your breathing slow. With each breath, remember: Christ is near to His people by His word and Spirit (John 14:26; John 15:7).  
If loneliness is present, do not hide it. The Lord sees affliction and hears the cry of His people (Psalm 34:17-18). [PAUSE 3s]

### 3. Scripture

John 15:4-5:

"Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me.  
I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing."

[PAUSE 3s]

### 4. Reflection

Jesus does not describe His people as machines that produce for Him. He describes them as branches living from the vine (John 15:5).  
A branch does not create life by trying harder. It bears fruit by remaining connected to the vine (John 15:4-5).

So today, do not begin with striving. Begin with abiding.  
Abide by listening to Christ's words (John 15:7). Abide by keeping His commands in love (John 15:10). Abide by receiving His love, for He says, "As the Father hath loved me, so have I loved you" (John 15:9). [PAUSE 3s]

Loneliness says, "I am cut off." Jesus says, "Abide in me" (John 15:4).  
Fruitfulness does not come from isolation or self-reliance. Jesus says, "without me ye can do nothing" (John 15:5).  
Let that be relief, not shame. You are invited to depend on Him.

### 5. Prayer

Lord Jesus, true Vine, keep me near to You (John 15:1, 4).  
Let Your words abide in me, and teach me to remain in Your love (John 15:7, 9).  
Where loneliness tempts me toward despair, draw me back to Your presence and Your people (John 15:4; Hebrews 10:24-25).  
Bear in me the fruit that glorifies the Father (John 15:8). Amen.

### 6. Rest in His Presence

Now rest in Christ.  
You do not need to manufacture spiritual life. The branch abides in the vine (John 15:4-5). [PAUSE 3s]  
Quietly repeat: "Abide in me, and I in you." [PAUSE 3s]

### 7. Close / Blessing

Return gently. Christ is the vine; His people are the branches (John 15:5).  
May His word remain in you, His love hold you, and His fruit grow in you for the Father's glory (John 15:7-8).

**Scripture citations:** John 15:1; John 15:4-10; John 14:26; Psalm 34:17-18; Hebrews 10:24-25.  
**Review notes:** No uncertain doctrine included. "Abiding" is framed from John 15 as dependence on Christ, not mystical self-absorption.
